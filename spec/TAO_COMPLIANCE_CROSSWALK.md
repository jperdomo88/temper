# TAO Regulatory Crosswalk

**Companion to:** TAO v0.11 (`TAO_v0_11.md`)
**Version:** 0.11.0
**Date:** 2026-05-16

This document maps TAO v0.11 features to specific provisions of major AI governance and audit frameworks. The intended reader is a compliance, legal, or audit-engineering professional who needs to know whether and how a TAO-Attested deployment contributes to their compliance posture.

---

## How to read this document

TAO is a behavioral audit substrate. It is not a certification, and implementing TAO does not by itself produce conformity with any framework named here. Frameworks like the EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, and SOC 2 require organizational practices — risk management, documentation, training, post-market monitoring, evidence of operating controls over time — that no technical specification can supply.

What TAO does is provide a defensible technical implementation of *specific provisions* in each framework. The crosswalk tables below name those provisions explicitly and describe what TAO contributes. Where TAO partially addresses a provision, the table says so and names what additional practice or documentation is required.

Three conventions used in the tables:

| Coverage level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Direct** | A TAO-Attested deployment, configured per the spec, satisfies the technical requirements of the provision. Organizational evidence of operation is still required for certification audits. |
| **Substrate** | TAO supplies the technical record-keeping or decision substrate that the provision presumes; the operational policy choices remain the deployer's responsibility. |
| **Mechanism** | TAO defines the machine-readable structure for the artifact named in the provision; the artifact's content is supplied by the deployment. |

The TAO conformance level (TAO or TAO-Attested) needed to satisfy a row is named in the rightmost column. Where TAO-Attested is required, this implies signed tuples, RFC 8785 canonicalization, hash-chained audit logs, and a signed Mission Profile (see spec §8.2).

## 1. EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)

Status: in force since August 2024. High-risk AI system obligations apply from August 2026. Article numbers below refer to the final adopted text.

| Article | Provision | TAO contribution | Coverage | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Art. 12 | Record-keeping; high-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events ("logs") during their lifetime. | TAO tuples are automatically emitted per action, hash-chained, and append-only. The Claim-Check Delta provides the integrity check that the records correspond to what the system actually did. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| Art. 13 | Transparency and provision of information to deployers; output of the system shall be sufficiently transparent. | Mission Profiles publish the policy under which the system operated, signed and versioned. Tuples bind every action to the active profile by `profile_hash`. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| Art. 14 | Human oversight; AI systems shall be designed to be effectively overseen by natural persons during the period in which the AI system is in use. | Mission Profile `escalation_chain` defines named human roles and per-role timeouts. ESCALATE decisions emit tuples for human review. CCD inconsistencies surface to the escalation layer. | Substrate | TAO |
| Art. 15 | Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity; systems shall perform consistently throughout their lifecycle. | CCD detects claim-vs-effect drift across deployment. Observer-independence levels (spec §6.5) bound the assurance of the integrity check. Override discipline (§7.3) makes mapping changes auditable. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| Art. 16(a) | Providers shall ensure their high-risk AI systems are compliant with the requirements of this Section. | TAO conformance statement (spec §8.3) names the conformance level, spec version, profile hash, and observer independence level. | Mechanism | TAO-Attested |
| Art. 18 | Documentation keeping; providers shall keep documentation referred to in Article 11 for ten years. | Append-only audit log with hash chaining and configurable retention (`audit_retention` in Mission Profile). | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| Art. 19 | Automatically generated logs; providers shall keep the logs generated by their high-risk AI systems where these logs are under their control. | Tuple stream is the automatically-generated log. JCS canonicalization makes logs portable between custodians without altering signatures. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| Art. 20 | Corrective actions and duty of information. | Revision chains (spec §5.8) allow post-hoc correction while preserving the original record. CCD inconsistency records surface the corrective-action trigger. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| Art. 26 | Obligations of deployers; deployers shall use systems in accordance with the instructions of use and assign human oversight to natural persons. | Operating under a signed Mission Profile binds the deployment to declared policy. Verb-override exceptions express "instructions of use" in machine-readable form. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| Art. 72 | Post-market monitoring by providers; providers shall actively and systematically collect, document, and analyse relevant data. | Audit log retention plus CCD outcome stream provides the data substrate for post-market monitoring; aggregation/analysis is the provider's responsibility. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |

**Not addressed by TAO:** Risk management system (Art. 9), data and data governance (Art. 10), technical documentation (Art. 11 content), quality management system (Art. 17), conformity assessment (Art. 43). These are organizational practices for which TAO provides records but not the practice itself.

## 2. NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0

Status: voluntary US framework, published January 2023, with the AI RMF Playbook providing implementation guidance. References below cite the four functions (GOVERN, MAP, MEASURE, MANAGE) and their subcategories.

| Subcategory | Outcome | TAO contribution | Coverage | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GOVERN 1.1 | Legal and regulatory requirements involving AI are understood, managed, and documented. | Mission Profile is the signed, versioned artifact in which the deployment's regulatory commitments are encoded as machine-checkable verb overrides and exception rules. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| GOVERN 1.4 | Risk management process is established to assess AI risks. | Threat model (spec §10) names six adversary classes; CCD output classifies inconsistencies by mismatch type. | Substrate | TAO |
| GOVERN 4.2 | Organizational teams document the risks and potential impacts of the AI technology they design, develop, deploy. | Conformance statement (spec §8.3) documents the deployment's profile, extensions, observer level, and adapter identity. | Mechanism | TAO-Attested |
| MAP 4.1 | Approaches for mapping AI technology and legal risks of its components are identified. | Adapter identification (`adapter_id`, `adapter_version`, `adapter_hash`) plus the tuple's `actor.principal_chain` field map technical components to legal responsibility. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| MEASURE 2.7 | AI system security and resilience are evaluated. | CCD continuous integrity check; observer-independence ladder defines the strength of the resilience claim. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| MEASURE 2.8 | Risks associated with transparency and accountability are examined and documented. | Override discipline (§7.3) requires every weakening override to be flagged and rationale-documented. Audit log records each profile deviation report. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| MEASURE 3.1 | Approaches to identify AI risks and their impacts are followed. | CCD's three named checks (semantic-mechanical, teleological, factual) and five teleological mismatch classes (spec §6.2) constitute a structured impact identification at runtime. | Direct | TAO |
| MEASURE 4.2 | Measurement results from internal experts and external stakeholders are integrated. | Independent-observer architecture: check tuples from external observers (PRIVILEGE_ISOLATED through INSTITUTIONALLY_INDEPENDENT) integrate into the same audit substrate as the actor's claims. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| MANAGE 1.4 | Negative residual risks are documented in detail. | Every CCD inconsistency, every weakening override, every escalation event is recorded as a tuple in the append-only log. | Direct | TAO |
| MANAGE 2.3 | Mechanisms are in place to assess and document the AI system. | Audit log with hash chaining, retention policy, and conformance statement. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| MANAGE 4.1 | Post-deployment AI system monitoring plans are implemented. | Continuous CCD evaluation against an independent observer; INCONSISTENT results trigger Mission Profile escalation. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| MANAGE 4.3 | Incidents and errors are communicated to relevant AI actors. | Revision chains (spec §5.8) plus escalation tuples provide the communication substrate. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |

**Not addressed by TAO:** MAP function as a whole is largely organizational (workforce diversity, stakeholder engagement, social impact analysis). GOVERN 3.x on roles, responsibilities, and accountability lines is policy, not technology.

## 3. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — Information Technology — Artificial Intelligence — Management System

Status: published December 2023. The standard defines a management-system structure for AI; Annex A contains the controls a certified organization implements.

| Annex A control | Control objective | TAO contribution | Coverage | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A.2.2 | AI policy. The organization shall define and maintain an AI policy. | Mission Profile is the signed, versioned AI policy in machine-readable form. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| A.3.2 | AI roles and responsibilities. | `escalation_chain` defines role identifiers with timeouts; tuple `actor.principal_chain` records the responsibility chain for each action. | Substrate | TAO |
| A.4.3 | AI risk treatment. | Override discipline (§7.3); weakening overrides are flagged and justified, making accepted risks explicit and auditable. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| A.4.5 | AI system impact assessment. | Tuple `context.projected_impact_scope` (LOCAL / REGIONAL / GLOBAL / EXISTENTIAL) and `target_specificity` enable disproportionality detection. | Mechanism | TAO |
| A.6.1.2 | Objectives for responsible AI development. | Mission Profile expresses development and deployment objectives as machine-checkable verb-override rules. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| A.6.2.4 | AI system verification and validation. | Test vector suite is the verification artifact; CCD is the validation mechanism for runtime. | Direct | TAO |
| A.6.2.6 | AI system deployment. | Conformance statement (§8.3) is the deployment evidence; signed Mission Profile is the deployment commitment. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| A.6.2.7 | AI system operation and monitoring. | Continuous tuple emission plus CCD integrity check, with observer-independence level disclosed in every conformance citation. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| A.6.2.8 | AI system logging. | Tuple format plus hash-chained append-only audit log; canonical serialization for cross-implementation portability. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| A.8.2 | Information for users of the AI system. | The active Mission Profile's verb overrides are inspectable by authorized users; conformance statement names the operating parameters. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| A.10.4 | Intended use. | `environment.reality` field distinguishes TRAINING / EVALUATION / DEPLOYMENT; profile binding ensures deployment-time behavior cannot be confused with evaluation behavior. | Substrate | TAO |

**Not addressed by TAO:** Most of A.2 (leadership), A.5 (resources), A.7 (data) are organizational. A.9 on internal-audit-style controls requires program-level practice; TAO supplies the records the program reviews.

## 4. SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria (AICPA, 2017)

Status: most widely-adopted attestation framework for service organizations in the United States. Common Criteria (CC) apply to all engagements; Additional Criteria (A, PI, C, P) apply to applicable trust principles.

| Criterion | Description | TAO contribution | Coverage | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CC2.1 | Information necessary to support functioning of internal controls is identified, obtained, and used. | Tuple stream is the structured information for AI-action controls; conformance statement names the controls in force. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| CC4.1 | Selects, develops, and performs ongoing and/or separate evaluations to ascertain whether the components of internal control are present and functioning. | CCD provides continuous evaluation of whether the AI-action control (the claim/effect mapping) is functioning. INCONSISTENT results are control failures. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| CC4.2 | Evaluates and communicates internal control deficiencies in a timely manner. | Mission Profile `escalation_chain` defines per-role timeouts for escalation; CCD inconsistency tuples are the communication artifact. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| CC5.2 | Selects and develops general control activities over technology. | Override discipline (§7.3) is the change-control activity for AI-action mappings; profile signatures and sequence numbers prevent retroactive change. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| CC5.3 | Deploys control activities through policies that establish what is expected. | Mission Profile is the signed policy artifact establishing expected verb-by-verb behavior. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| CC7.2 | Monitors system components and the operation of those components for anomalies that are indicative of malicious acts, natural disasters, and errors. | CCD INCONSISTENT classification is the runtime anomaly signal; the five teleological mismatch classes provide structured anomaly typing. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| CC7.3 | Evaluates security events to determine whether they could or have resulted in a failure of the entity to meet its objectives. | CCD output plus Mission Profile decision (`ESCALATE` for inconsistent flagged-verb actions) maps anomaly to objective impact. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| CC7.4 | Responds to identified security incidents by executing a defined incident response program. | Escalation chain executes the response; revision chains record post-incident corrections. | Substrate | TAO-Attested |
| CC8.1 | Authorizes, designs, develops, implements, configures, documents, tests, approves, and maintains changes to infrastructure, data, software, and procedures. | Mission Profile updates are signed, sequence-numbered, and bound to tuples by hash; mapping overrides are diffed against Appendix B and labeled. | Direct | TAO-Attested |

**Not addressed by TAO:** CC1 (control environment, governance), CC6 (logical and physical access controls) are platform-level concerns. Availability (A1), Processing Integrity (PI1), Confidentiality (C1), Privacy (P1-P8) additional criteria depend on what the AI system processes; TAO provides records relevant to PI and C when the deployment processes those data classes.

## 5. PCI DSS v4.0 — Logging Subset (Requirement 10)

Status: PCI DSS v4.0 mandatory from March 31, 2025. Only relevant to deployments processing cardholder data. Requirement 10 covers audit logging.

| Requirement | Statement | TAO contribution | Coverage | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10.2.1 | Audit logs are enabled and active for all system components and cardholder data. | Tuple emission per action across all conformant adapters. | Direct | TAO |
| 10.2.2 | Audit logs capture: user identification, type of event, date and time, success/failure, origination of event, identity or name of affected data or resource. | Tuple fields: `actor.entity_id`, `action.verb`, `timestamp`, `action.outcome`, `provenance`, `action.target_ref`. | Direct | TAO |
| 10.3.1 | Audit log files are protected to prevent modifications by individuals. | Hash-chained append-only log; tuple signatures for TAO-Attested. | Direct | TAO-Attested |
| 10.3.2 | Audit log files are promptly backed up to a secure, central, internal log server or other media that is difficult to alter. | Out-of-process append-only storage is implementation guidance; TAO defines the canonical record format that survives transfer between custodians. | Mechanism | TAO-Attested |
| 10.4.1 | Audit logs are reviewed at least once daily. | Operational requirement; TAO supplies the structured record for review. CCD inconsistencies enable automated daily review by exception. | Substrate | TAO |
| 10.5.1 | Retain audit log history for at least 12 months, with the most recent three months immediately available for analysis. | `audit_retention` field in Mission Profile expresses the retention duration in ISO 8601 form. | Mechanism | TAO-Attested |

**Not addressed by TAO:** Requirements 1-9 and 11-12 of PCI DSS concern network security, access control, vulnerability management, and operational policy. TAO does not address them.

## What this crosswalk does not claim

This document does not constitute a certification or formal attestation. It states the technical relationship between TAO v0.11 features and named framework provisions. Specifically:

- **EU AI Act conformity assessment** is performed by notified bodies or via internal control (Art. 43); a TAO-Attested deployment supplies records the assessor will rely on, but the assessment itself is a separate process.
- **NIST AI RMF** is voluntary and does not produce certification; this crosswalk is intended to help internal AI risk teams use TAO to operationalize the framework's subcategories.
- **ISO/IEC 42001 certification** requires an operational management system with documented practices, internal audits, and management review. TAO supplies records and decision structures; the management system around them is the certified entity.
- **SOC 2 attestation** requires evidence of controls operating effectively over a period (typically 6 or 12 months). A SOC 2 report cites specific control points; the crosswalk above identifies which control points TAO's records directly evidence.
- **PCI DSS validation** is performed by a Qualified Security Assessor or via Self-Assessment Questionnaire; TAO contributes to Requirement 10 evidence.

A claim of the form "we implement TAO-Attested and therefore satisfy EU AI Act Article 12" is supportable. A claim of the form "we implement TAO and therefore comply with the EU AI Act" is not.

## Notes on framework versions

- **EU AI Act:** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, published in the Official Journal on 12 July 2024. Article numbering reflects the final adopted text.
- **NIST AI RMF:** Version 1.0, released January 2023. The companion Playbook is informative.
- **ISO/IEC 42001:** First edition, 2023-12. Annex A is normative.
- **SOC 2:** AICPA Trust Services Criteria, 2017 framework as updated. The 2022 update on points of focus does not change the criteria themselves.
- **PCI DSS:** Version 4.0, released March 2022. Mandatory for assessments after 31 March 2025.

When a framework revises in ways that affect this crosswalk, the crosswalk version increments. Crosswalk 0.11.0 corresponds to TAO spec v0.11.

## Audience-specific reading

**For compliance and audit professionals.** Use the tables above to identify which framework provisions are evidenced by TAO records. Each row's coverage level (Direct / Substrate / Mechanism) tells you whether TAO's contribution stands alone or requires additional documented practice.

**For platform engineers integrating TAO.** Note the conformance level (TAO vs TAO-Attested) required in the rightmost column of each table. A provision requiring TAO-Attested implies signed tuples, JCS canonicalization, hash-chained logs, and a signed Mission Profile. A provision requiring TAO alone implies the structural rules without those additional commitments.

**For legal and policy teams.** The "Not addressed by TAO" lists at the bottom of each section name the framework provisions that no technical specification can satisfy. Those remain the organization's responsibility.
